No signal. Still paid.

Payment infrastructure for Africa's informal traders. A market woman gets paid with no network and no smartphone, and every day she trades becomes the credit history a bank can lend against.

The problem

Market traders, most of them women, run real businesses that leave almost no financial record. Their sales are cash or patchy mobile transfers that fail when the network drops. Many of their customers, and many traders themselves, use basic phones.

With no formal credit history, they can't get affordable credit, so stock, growth and emergencies run on cash or expensive informal loans.

What PayVault does

Pays offlineA customer's phone signs a payment with no network. The trader's phone verifies it on the spot. It settles automatically when either phone gets signal.
Turns sales into creditSettled sales count as trading days. Thirty days of trading lifts her score and unlocks an overdraft from the partner bank.
Works on any phoneA stall sticker (QR or NFC tag) customers pay by scanning or tapping, a USSD menu for basic phones, and SMS confirmations. English and French.

A sale, start to finish

  1. The customer scans or taps the trader's stall sticker, enters the amount and a PIN. No network needed.
  2. Her phone signs the payment from a pre-funded offline vault, with optional overdraft.
  3. When signal returns, the payment settles and the trader gets an SMS, even on a basic phone.
  4. Each trading day feeds her score. The bank sees a verified sales history and offers credit.

Why it's safe to offer offline

We don't claim offline double-spending is impossible. We make it bounded (small vault caps, per-payment and per-merchant limits), detected (every payment is hash-chained, so a cloned phone shows up as a fork) and recovered (a risk pool pays the merchant in full, then recovers from the ID-verified holder). A lost phone can be frozen from another device.

Business model

B2B infrastructure. Banks, mobile money operators and fintechs hold the licences and the funds. PayVault is the layer underneath, white-labelled into their apps.

  • Fee on each settled offline payment
  • Share of overdraft fees on credit it unlocks
  • Platform licence per partner and country
